Your brief

The pulse reads this on every run. Tell it what you're working on, who reads your stuff, and your hard rules. Markdown is fine. Vague briefs produce generic drafts.

Write this yourself — don't paste AI-polished prose. Introduce who you are, what you're building, and what you're trying to do. The brief itself is the voice anchor — Ling mirrors how you write here when drafting replies, so your cadence, casual phrasing, and even typos become the drafts' cadence. If the brief reads like a press release, every draft will too.

Saved to config.json (field: brief) and delivered to the agent on every chat open. Resetting reverts to brief.example.md shipped with the skill.

Workspace

Where the product you're launching lives on disk. Pulse reads the README, doc/, and source on every run so drafts cite real features instead of guessing — the difference between "excited to ship a new feature!" and writing that sounds like you. Optional but high-leverage.

Read-only access. Pulse never writes to the workspace. Saved to config.json as workspace_path.

Auto-compact threshold

Pulse pulls full Reddit threads (OP + every comment) during Gather Web, which fills the agent's context fast. Lower thresholds compact earlier — at the cost of more summarization passes; higher thresholds keep verbatim history longer at the cost of bigger turns. The engine default is 95%; Pulse defaults to 50%.

% of context window

Saved to config.json as compact_threshold (0.10 – 0.99). Applied on every iframe load.

Websites

One toggle per site. Enable it and Pulse fetches from it, surfaces mentions and discovery, and drafts replies for it. Pure publishing lanes (Blog, Medium, LinkedIn, Substack) just toggle on as draft formats (constraints in references/lane-templates.md). Nothing is ever auto-posted — every draft waits for your review.

Custom URLs are added as RSS / Atom feeds (source-only). Pulse reads them via the same fetcher used for the built-in RSS source.
