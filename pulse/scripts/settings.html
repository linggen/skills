Your brief

The pulse reads this on every run. Tell it what you're working on, who reads your stuff, and your hard rules. Markdown is fine. Vague briefs produce generic drafts.

Write this yourself — don't paste AI-polished prose. Introduce who you are, what you're building, and what you're trying to do. The brief itself is the voice anchor — Ling mirrors how you write here when drafting replies, so your cadence, casual phrasing, and even typos become the drafts' cadence. If the brief reads like a press release, every draft will too.

Saved to config.json (field: brief) and delivered to the agent on every chat open. Resetting reverts to brief.example.md shipped with the skill.

Workspace

Where your work lives on disk. Pulse reads it read-only: commits for the progress card, and your product's README + latest CHANGELOG entry for the product digest that rides every drafting goal — the difference between "excited to ship a new feature!" and a draft that names the thing you actually built. Optional but high-leverage.

Root. Saved as workspace_path — also the read grant and the dir scanned for commits.

Product repos — one path per line, up to 6. The repos that describe what you're launching; each one's README and latest CHANGELOG entry goes into the digest. Absolute, ~/…, or relative to the root above. A launch is often several repos (app, CLI, site) and the sentence that answers a thread may be in any of them. Leave empty to read the root itself.

Read-only access. Pulse never writes to the workspace. Saved to config.json as product_repos.

Mentions

How drafts may name your product. On a thread where the product is the direct answer, a disclosed draft answers on the merits first, then adds one sentence — "I built <product> (<site>) for this — it does X" — with the site as plain text, never a link. Implicit never names it. The self-promotion ratio is Reddit's rule (1 in 10 by default); Pulse counts it from your own recent comments and forces implicit when the budget is spent. Every draft carries the register it used.

% of your recent comments that may name it (counted per 10)

Saved to config.json as mention. Empty product = implicit everywhere.

Auto-compact threshold

Pulse pulls full Reddit threads (OP + every comment) during Gather Web, which fills the agent's context fast. Higher thresholds keep verbatim history longer at the cost of bigger turns; lower thresholds compact earlier to save context. The engine default is 95%; Pulse defaults to 70%.

Don't go below ~60%. Gather Web reads several full threads in one pass; if compaction fires during a gather it summarizes those reads away before the agent can draft from them, so Discovery and Mentions come back empty (the agent can't ground a draft on data that's gone). 60–80% leaves room for a gather to finish first.

% of context window

Saved to config.json as compact_threshold (0.10 – 0.99). Applied on every iframe load.

Websites

One toggle per site. Enable it and Pulse fetches from it, surfaces mentions and discovery, and drafts replies for it. Pure publishing lanes (Blog, Medium, LinkedIn, Substack) just toggle on as draft formats (constraints in references/lane-templates.md). Nothing is ever auto-posted — every draft waits for your review.

Custom URLs are added as RSS / Atom feeds (source-only). Pulse reads them via the same fetcher used for the built-in RSS source.
