Two tabs: what stood out, and everything else

Apple Health holds hundreds of measurements and shows you all of them. Linggen Health reads every one of yours each night, against your own history, and builds one screen out of what it found. Nothing wrong is still a screen — it just is not a warning.

The phone had five tabs, which is the app printing its own table of contents along the bottom of every view. Now it has two. Highlights is composed each morning by the agent out of your whole history — not out of today. The examination reads years; what reaches this page is the few things that stood out, and something that started nine days ago leads it just as readily as something that started this morning. Four cards at most, read in seconds. Everything is the drawer you open when you want something specific, and every row in it says how much is behind it. A Mac keeps the full tab strip, because a Mac has room. Pick a person below. All figures are examples.

Highlights

Four cards at most, drawn from all of it

The examination reads every measurement this person has ever recorded, and what earns a place is whatever is still true: a shift that began nine days ago leads this page every morning until it passes, and every chart on it is drawn over weeks. The number is the one thing here that is about today — it is today against this person's own normal, it is not a health score, and it says what it was made of. Under it: the finding, if there is one, with how long it has held; what you actually did; and the one measurement you are working on. What a workout was, not that it happened

Apple already tells you a workout took place. What it does not do is put it beside your last five of the same kind, which is the only way to know whether it was a good one. The nightly examination

Every measurement this person has, judged against their own history

One job, run each night, walks every type in the store. Most come back with nothing to say. What it produces is one of three verdicts, and only two of them ever reach the screen. Sustained is the word that keeps it useful: one low morning is a rest day, nine days of a shifted baseline is something else.

At your normal

The measurement sits inside its usual spread. It is not shown at all, and it is not a card taking up a slot. It counts toward the status line, which is where "everything is fine" gets said once.

—
Worth seeing

Moved enough to change what today should be, or drifting week over week. It appears with its evidence and what it changes. It leaves on its own when the number comes back.

—
Worth a doctor

A sustained adverse shift against this person's own baseline, or one of a short list of published red flags. Always on top, never collapsed, never hidden by a tap, and it never names a condition.

The charts

Four forms, and one of them gets the space

A chart is not one per measurement. A candidate is a metric × a form, because the same number drawn the wrong way lies — and the app carries four forms, one per kind of question. Every judged type pairs with the forms it can carry: the list is the judging table, not a second list. What reaches the screen is one of them, picked the way the index has always been picked, plus the week bars, which answer a different question and are always there.

Form C exists because a day total lies until the day ends: 560 kcal against a normal of 703 is not a low day at 10am, and a bare number cannot say so. A candidate whose form cannot be drawn — two points is not a line — is dropped with a reason rather than degraded into a worse chart.

Which chart

Picked for the person, not fixed in the app

Each candidate is ranked on three things: whether there is enough recent data, whether it means anything for who this person is, and whether it has actually moved — then dropped if its form cannot be drawn, or if it repeats what another candidate already says. A fixed list would put a misleading number on someone's screen. The clearest case is BMI, which reads a muscular lifter as overweight because it cannot tell muscle from fat.

Rules pick; the agent names and may overrule. The pass runs at 02:00 on a phone that may be offline, so the choice must be makeable with no model at all — and it must be stable, or nobody learns where anything is, and auditable, which this table is and a model's answer is not. What the agent is better at is the sentence, breaking a near-tie, vetoing a pick that is technically right and contextually silly, and honouring show me sleep instead. Its override is written down like any other layout change.

What you ate

Roughly, and then you correct it

Nothing writes food to Apple Health for most people, so the intake half of energy is missing and the calorie target on the body page is a number nobody can measure against. A sentence or a photo closes it. The shipping apps in this category land within 11–25% depending on how crowded the plate is, and the whole difference is portion — a photo flattens a bowl and the model has to guess how deep it goes. The ones that do best measure it instead, using the phone's depth sensor to get a shape and a volume. This iPhone has one. Depth can only be captured at the moment the photo is taken, so it is captured whether or not the first version uses it.

An estimate wears a different coat from a measurement, everywhere. Its own source, never mixed into the Apple Health rows, never charted as though it were measured, and never given a verdict — the examination judges measurements against your own history, and a guess has no business raising worth seeing. It shows a range until you correct it; after that it is your number and it is shown as yours. And the agent does not ask you about it: follow-up questions about a meal test as friction that does not improve the answer.

Against a 3,368 kcal target derived from a weigh-in, an estimate inside 20% is the difference between a number you can steer by and no number at all. Week over week the errors partly cancel, so the trend is worth more than any single plate.

The cost is a promise. The app says only the sentences you and the agent exchange reach a model, and a dinner photo is not a sentence. So the photo goes to your own ChatGPT, over the account you connect — your data, your relationship, and Linggen's servers stay out of the picture. The cloud model we supply is text-only and stays that way; a sentence always works, and a sentence is about as accurate as a photo without depth.

Depth is the division of labour. An image arrives at the model resized to about 800×800: enough to tell chicken from fish and see there is rice, nowhere near enough to know how deep the bowl is. The depth frame measures; the model identifies.

Everything else

She comes to you — the screen never has to

Yinyue's thread is one conversation for every Linggen app, and it is where she reports her own work: what she read last night, how much of it there was, and the one thing that came out. Nothing was asked for that first message. It is why the health screen can be as quiet as it is — the explaining happens somewhere you can answer back, and the screen is left holding findings. Behind it the plan, the checklist, the years of history and every one of the hundreds of measurements are all still there and none of it is on screen. The companion who already answers about your music and your money answers about your body from the same fourteen tools.

The Mac · optional

Same first view, and then everything else within reach

The first view here is composed by the same pass and leads with the same thing the phone led with. What a Mac adds is not more on that first screen: it is that everything is reachable behind it. The Data tab below is live — every measurement examined last night, including the thirty-odd that had nothing to say, so nothing needs you can be checked instead of believed. A phone never shows that list; it would be the confusion we started by removing.

The conversation sits on the right at the width every Linggen app uses, and it opens with Ling rather than with you: the pass finished at 02:14, this is what it found, and this is why the page is in this order. After that you ask; the answer comes back in a sentence or two and the working goes on the page beside it.

The words

Build the capability; do not claim the title

A daily examination of everything, an index chosen per person, a warning that will not be dismissed: all of that ships. What changes is the sentence on the screen and in the App Store listing. A diagnosis claim from an app that has not been cleared is a review rejection in the United States and a regulated medical device in the EU, and a user who believes the app is their doctor may not go to a real one.

What it says

  • "Your resting heart rate has been 12 above your normal for nine days."
  • "That has held long enough to be worth showing a doctor."
  • "HRV is 7 below your normal, so today is an easy day."
  • "Examined 38 measurements. Nothing needs you today."

What it never says

  • Any condition, by name or by hint.
  • A score of health, as though 82 out of 100 were a fact about a body.
  • "Normal" or "abnormal" against a population range instead of this person's own history.
  • Anything about a medication, a dose, or a clinical record it was given as context.
Rules

What keeps a quiet screen honest

Two tabs, and one of them is composed

A tab is a promise you will want that view often enough to pay for it with permanent screen. Only one view earns that: today. Everything else is a drawer, in a fixed order, with a count on every row.

Four cards, never more

The dashboard is read in seconds or it is not read. A fifth card is the agent failing to choose, and choosing is what it is for.

Quiet means no warning, not an empty screen

A measurement at your normal never gets a slot of its own. But the space a finding would have taken goes to your week and the one thing you are working on, because that is what you opened the app to see.

The first page is not about today

The examination reads all of it. Something that began nine days ago and has not passed leads the page every morning until it does, and it says how long it has held rather than arriving as this morning's news. Only the number is today's.

It comes to you

When a pass finds something, the agent says so in the conversation without being asked, and says what it did about it. A finding that waits for the user to open the right screen is a finding the app kept to itself.

The agent talks in the conversation, not on the screen

That it built the view, why it is in this order, and the offer to lay it out again — all of it is said where you can answer back. The screen carries findings and nothing the agent wants to tell you about itself.

Same first view, different depth

Both devices lead with the most important part and nothing else. A Mac keeps the full tab strip because it has room; a phone puts the same set one drawer down.

A session is a comparison, not a receipt

Apple already told you a workout happened. What earns a screen is the same session beside your last five of its kind — tonnage against your usual pulls, heart rate at a pace you have run before.

The quiet is stated, never implied

The status line says how many measurements were examined. An empty screen with no line reads as a broken app.

A warning outranks the quiet rule

It cannot be collapsed, hidden, or pushed below anything. Everything else on the screen can be.

Absent is never zero

No scale means no weight trend, not a weight of nothing. A denied permission and an unused sensor look identical to us, and we say so.

Your own baseline, never a population

A resting heart rate of 58 is flagged by a population rule and is nothing to worry about. The comparison is always to your own last month.

Sustained, not single

One reading changes today's session. Nine days of a shifted baseline is what raises a warning.

Every number shows its formula

Two grams per kilo of a weight measured on a date you can see. Change the body fact and the target re-derives in front of you.

It stays on your devices

Your phone, and your Mac if you have one. Only the sentences you and the agent exchange reach a model. Never iCloud, never sold.
