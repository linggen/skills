# Appendix · The Scroll of Breath

The broken bamboo scroll Yinyue left behind. The scroll is an old thing from Qingqiu, and it records words by itself: whenever someone in the world explains the art of breathing out and in well, it takes in a passage, which is why the scroll holds a jade inscription from the Warring States as well as a Song-dynasty alchemy classic. The notes are Yinyue's words, and they were not written all at once either — each time she wakes, a few more lines show on the scroll, and the people and things the notes speak of are what she saw while she was awake.


### Opening · What Breathing Out and In Is

> Blowing and puffing, breathing out and breathing in, spitting out the old and taking in the new, hanging like a bear and stretching like a bird — all this is only for long life. — *Zhuangzi*, "Constrained in Will"

**Yinyue's note**: "Spit out the old, take in the new" — breathe out what is old and muddy in the body, breathe in what is new and clear between heaven and earth.
That is where the words "breathing out and in" come from. Zhuangzi says that doing only this is good for long life and nothing more. This queen says: first a long life, then we'll talk about the rest.

> The true man breathes from his heels; the common man breathes from his throat. — *Zhuangzi*, "The Great and Venerable Teacher"

**Yinyue's note**: When ordinary people breathe, the breath stops at the throat; a person on the Way sinks one breath all the way down to the heels.
Where does your breath reach now? — The throat. Mm. Start practising from the throat.

### Part One · Moving the Qi (the oldest piece)

> Moving the qi: deep, then it gathers; gathered, it stretches; stretched, it goes down; down, it settles; settled, it is firm; firm, it sprouts; sprouted, it grows; grown, it returns; returned, it is heaven.
> Heaven's pulse beats above; earth's pulse beats below. Follow it and live; go against it and die.
> — *The Jade Pendant Inscription on Moving Qi* (Warring States, cut on a small twelve-sided jade column, forty-five characters counting the repeated ones, formerly held by the Tianjin History Museum; reading after the Wikipedia entry "Inscription on Moving Qi", checked 2026-10-01. The two lines "Heaven's pulse beats above; earth's pulse beats below" are read differently by different scholars)

**Yinyue's note**: This is the oldest breathing formula in the world. Over two thousand years ago someone cut it into jade, afraid it would be lost.
A breath drawn in must be **deep**; only when it is deep can it **gather** and stay; once it is full it will **stretch** out on its own and go **down**,
sinking to the bottom of the belly and **settling** there; settled, it becomes **firm**; firm, it **sprouts** like a seed and **grows** day by day;
when it has grown full, it **returns** along the way it came, all the way to the crown of the head, and reaches **heaven**.
Keep to this order and it is the road to life; scramble it and it will kill you. — So don't hurry.
An ordinary person who gets as far as "down" in one winter has already worked hard: the breath sinks, hands and feet warm up, the heart slows, and you can carry a load on a shoulder pole without panting.
For the six words after that, an ordinary body has no door. Where the door is, this queen knows.

### The Six Sounds of Breathing Out

> There is one way of taking breath in and six ways of letting it out. The one way in is inhaling; the six ways out are chui, hu, xi, he, xu and si, which all let breath out. … Chui to drive out heat, hu to drive out wind, xi to drive out vexation, he to bring the breath down, xu to scatter what is stuck, si to relieve exhaustion. People who are utterly exhausted mostly breathe xu and si.
> — Tao Hongjing, *On Nourishing Life*, the chapter on Taking In Qi to Cure Illness, quoting the *Classic of Taking In Qi* (transcribed in simplified characters; original text on Wikisource, *Yangxing Yanming Lu*, second scroll)

**Yinyue's note**: Breathing in has only one door; breathing out has six. Ordinary people think breathing out is throwing something away, done once it's gone; in fact the out-breath matters more than the in-breath.
The six sounds each shape the mouth differently; what they share is that you narrow the breath and let it out thin and long. Breathe out slowly and the heart slows; once the heart is slow, a breath can be held longer.
Chui is cool, for blowing on porridge; he is warm, for warming your hands; si is tired — a person tired to the very end needs no teaching, they sigh by themselves.

### Between Breathing Out and In

> Breathing out comes from the heart and lungs; breathing in goes to the kidneys and liver; between breathing out and in, the spleen receives the taste of grain, and its pulse lies in the middle.
> — *Classic of Difficult Issues*, the Fourth Difficulty (transcribed in simplified characters; original text on Wikisource, *Nanjing*)

**Yinyue's note**: A breath that stops at the chest isn't finished. It has to sink all the way down below the small of the back before it counts as taken in.
And don't be greedy, gulping one breath after another — empty the body of all its muddy air and you get just as dizzy, your fingers go just as numb. Leave a little of the muddy air too.
Mortal medical books sometimes talk sense as well.

### Part Two · Holding to the One

> Carrying the body and soul and embracing the One, can you keep them from parting? Concentrating the breath until it is soft, can you be like a newborn child? — *Daodejing*, chapter ten
> Reach emptiness to the utmost; hold stillness steadfastly. The ten thousand things rise together, and I watch them return. — *Daodejing*, chapter sixteen

**Yinyue's note**: Body and soul held together, not parting — that is called "embracing the One." The breath should be single and soft,
soft as a newborn baby's. A baby doesn't have to learn; it is born knowing how to breathe with its belly. You grow up and forget.
When you practise, the heart has to be empty all the way down and still all the way down. The moment you think about Ma Xiaobao, your breath scatters. — You can think about him. Think after you've practised.

### Part Three · True Qi

> Calm, content, empty and still, the true qi follows; essence and spirit are kept within — so where would illness come from? — *The Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic*, Plain Questions, "On the True Nature of High Antiquity"
> Breathe in the essence of qi, stand alone and guard the spirit, and the flesh becomes as one. — the same

**Yinyue's note**: Only when the heart doesn't grasp or hurry, and is empty, will the **true qi** follow you; when essence and spirit are kept inside the body and don't run out,
illness can't get in. — Your father's knees are half injury and half a lifetime of hurrying.

### Part Four · Womb Breathing (the end of Qi Condensation)

> One who attains womb breathing can breathe without nose or mouth, as if in the womb, and then the Way is complete.
> When you first learn to move the qi, draw the qi in through the nose and hold it, count silently in your heart to a hundred and twenty, then let it out gently through the mouth;
> both letting it out and drawing it in, do not let your own ears hear the sound of the qi going out and in; always let more go in than comes out.
> Put a goose feather on the nose and mouth; when you breathe out and the feather does not stir, that is the sign.
> Little by little raise the count in your heart; after long practice you can reach a thousand.
> — Ge Hong, *Baopuzi*, Inner Chapters, "Resolving Hindrances"

**Yinyue's note**: This is the most solid piece of all. Breathe in once through the nose, hold it, count silently to a hundred and twenty, then let it out through the mouth a thread at a time —
breathing in and out so lightly that even your own ears can't hear it, taking in more than you let out. Put a goose feather in front of your nose,
and only when it doesn't stir at all as you breathe out have you stepped through the door.
At the very end you need neither nose nor mouth; like a child in its mother's womb, you can "breathe" by yourself — and that is womb breathing.
**The end of the ninth layer of Qi Condensation is womb breathing.** Only at that step can you establish your foundation.

### Close · Where the Road Goes

> The work of stillness lies in a single moment, and within that single moment is the work of refining essence into qi, refining qi into spirit, and refining spirit back into emptiness. — *The Complete Works of Master Zhang Sanfeng*, scroll three
> And there are the proofs of the work: a hundred days to lay the foundation, ten months for the womb to ripen, three years of nursing. — *What the Way of Heavenly Immortals Forbids*

**Yinyue's note**: The pill-making Way of the world splits the whole road into three stretches: refine the essence in the body into qi; refine the qi into spirit;
refine the spirit back into emptiness. What you're doing now is the first step of the first stretch — **Qi Condensation**.
The hundred days of "a hundred days to lay the foundation" is an alchemist's round figure; don't take it seriously and count the days. There's a long way still to go. This queen is waiting for you up ahead.

### The Nine Layers of Qi Condensation (each layer in the game)

| Layer | Name | Work (daily) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| First layer | **Drawing Qi** — the dantian first warms | Count to 120; the feather does not stir on the out-breath (the sign of entry) | *Baopuzi*, the feather as the sign |
| Second layer | **Gathering Qi** — the breath can be held | A quarter-hour of breathing at midnight, the breath sunk to the belly; count to 200 | Moving the Qi, "deep", "gathers" |
| Third layer | **Moving Qi** — the breath goes where the mind sends it | The breath travels from the belly out to the four limbs | Moving the Qi, "stretches", "down" |
| Fourth layer | **Settling Qi** — sitting still, it does not scatter | Sit for one stick of incense without the heart stirring | *Daodejing*, hold stillness steadfastly |
| Fifth layer | **Firm Qi** — no illness gets in | Count to 500 | *Inner Classic*, the true qi follows |
| Sixth layer | **Soft Qi** — concentrate the breath until soft | The breath soft as a baby's; belly breathing | *Daodejing*, concentrating the breath until soft |
| Seventh layer | **Breath to the Heels** — one breath down to the heels | The breath sinks to the heels | *Zhuangzi*, breathing from the heels |
| Eighth layer | **To a Thousand** — count to a thousand | Count to a thousand; the feather does not stir | *Baopuzi*, "after long practice you can reach a thousand" |
| Ninth layer | **Womb Breathing** — not through mouth or nose | Sitting still, breathing by itself → Foundation Establishment possible | *Baopuzi*, womb breathing |
